## Usability heuristics (Nielsen's 10)

A fast, structured way to find usability problems without user testing.

1. **Visibility of system status** — always show what's happening (loading, saved, progress).
2. **Match the real world** — use familiar words/concepts, logical order, not system jargon.
3. **User control & freedom** — provide undo/cancel and clear exits ("emergency exit").
4. **Consistency & standards** — follow platform conventions; same thing = same word/look.
5. **Error prevention** — prevent mistakes (constraints, confirmations, good defaults)
   rather than just reporting them.
6. **Recognition over recall** — show options and context; don't make users remember.
7. **Flexibility & efficiency** — shortcuts/accelerators for experts; sensible defaults
   for novices.
8. **Aesthetic & minimalist design** — remove the irrelevant; every extra element competes.
9. **Help users with errors** — plain-language messages that explain and offer a fix.
10. **Help & documentation** — provide it when needed, searchable, task-focused.

### How to run a review
- Walk the main user flow screen by screen.
- For each screen, note any heuristic that is violated and the specific element.
- Rank issues by severity (blocker / major / minor / cosmetic) and propose a fix.

### Review questions
- Does the user always know the current state and what happens next?
- Can they recover from mistakes easily?
- Is anything on screen not pulling its weight?
