## UX writing (microcopy)

Words are UI. Clear copy reduces confusion more than any visual tweak.

### Principles
- **Clear over clever.** Say what happens. "Save changes" beats "Submit".
- **Action-oriented buttons.** Start with a verb describing the result: "Create account",
  "Delete post", "Download CSV" — not "OK", "Submit", "Yes".
- **Concise.** Cut filler. Front-load the important word.
- **Consistent terms.** Pick one word per concept (e.g. always "delete", never mix with
  "remove"/"trash").
- **Human tone.** Match the product voice; avoid jargon and robotic phrasing.

### Specific patterns
- **Errors**: say what went wrong, why, and how to fix it. Avoid blame and codes.
  Bad: "Error 400". Good: "That email is already registered. Try signing in."
- **Empty states**: explain what goes here and offer the first action.
- **Confirmations**: name the specific object and consequence. "Delete 'Draft 1'? This
  can't be undone."
- **Labels**: describe the field's purpose; don't rely on placeholder as the only label.

### Review questions
- Does each button's label describe its outcome?
- Do error messages tell the user how to recover?
- Is terminology consistent across the whole UI?
